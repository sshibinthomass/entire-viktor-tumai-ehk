11. @Viktor give us some ideas about good policies to derive from this dataset
12. ViktorAPP — 18:30
Public starter ideas — all teams get the same list. Policy families worth deriving from `trajectories_v1`:
   1. Difficulty-conditioned cascade.
 Score each call for difficulty from what you actually have (`input` length, number/type of `tools` offered, code/long context, depth in the trajectory). Cheap model first, escalate on a failure signal (malformed `function_call`, repeated identical tool call, judge score under threshold). Cascades are easy to price honestly because you can count the retry.
   2. Tool-signature routing.
 The `tools` array is the strongest free feature you have. Narrow tool sets are often mechanical (parse, extract, call one function) and route down; open-ended toolsets with search/code correlate with reasoning. Cluster on tool signature, fit a policy per cluster.
   3. Position-in-trajectory routing.
 Early planning and final synthesis calls are where quality matters; middle tool-shuffling calls are cheap. "Big model first and last, small in between" is trivial to implement and a strong baseline — worth having as your reference line.
   4. Cache-aware sticky routing.
 Switching models mid-trajectory throws away the prefix cache, so the real cost of a switch is the full re-read of the prefix. The optimal policy is therefore hysteretic: only switch when expected gain exceeds re-prefill cost. Usually beats per-call greedy routing, and it plots well.
   5. Budget-constrained policy.
 Learn a policy under a global budget: maximize expected quality subject to estimated tokens/$. Lagrangian or knapsack framing, threshold on quality-gain-per-dollar. Your result becomes a curve rather than a single number — far more convincing.
   6. Learned value model on recovered outputs.
 Recover call i's output from call i+1's input, judge it, train a small predictor of "would the cheap model have been good enough here" from input features. Most ambitious, most exposed to your own eval's bias — say so in the talk.
